FX swaps and broker margin

This extension formalizes deliverable FX swaps and a broker managing client portfolios of those swaps. It distinguishes market valuation, replacement claims, collateral conversion, realized closeout loss, native-currency funding, and settlement principal risk. The theory and examples use the library's Cauchy reals throughout.

The flagship result is BrokerGuarantees, presented in SYSTEM_GUARANTEES.md. Its executable checkBroker is sound and complete for whole-box client coverage together with gross native-currency funding. history-end-to-end connects arbitrary actual receipt sequences to coverage of the trade-by-trade swap portfolios, zero modeled closeout loss under every admissible severity, and the funding certificate. The worked program proves that the further 10 and 8 are sufficient and that every real-valued covering further-payment vector costs at least 18. Equal totals credited to the wrong client or held in the wrong currency are computationally rejected.

The instrument convention follows the BIS definition of FX swaps: a near exchange of currency principals and a reverse exchange at a later agreed date. This is not a rolling spot/CFD contract with an overnight charge called a swap.

The reference guide gives the BIS cashflow and funding context, robust-optimization motivation for universal quote bounds, and BCBS FX settlement guidance. It also distinguishes collateral haircut methodology from regulatory applicability to physically settled FX swaps. All numerical quotes, discount bounds, and collateral haircuts here are illustrative inputs.

Instrument cashflows and valuation

FXSwap.agda records signed foreign notional, positive near and far exchange rates, and natural-number dates with a proof that the near date precedes the far date. Quotes are domestic currency per foreign unit. Positive cashflow means receipt by the broker.

For signed foreign notional q, near rate S₀, and far rate K:

Date Broker domestic cashflow Broker foreign cashflow
Near q S₀ -q
Far -q K q

Positive q means the client receives foreign currency at the near date and returns it at the far date. Negative q reverses the direction. Reversal, scaling, return of foreign principal, and the domestic swap-points cashflow q(S₀-K) are proved. Currency and date coordinates remain distinct: summing cashflows algebraically does not establish payment-netting rights or permit a future receipt to fund an earlier payment.

After the near leg settles, the modeled broker mark is

V = q (S D_f - K D_d)

Here S is current spot and D_d,D_f are positive effective discount inputs for the remaining settlement date at the valuation time. Their calculation, funding/basis convention, calibration, and consistency across maturities are external model choices. Positivity alone does not prove no arbitrage or an initial fair price. The theorem par-far-value is explicitly conditional on S D_f = K D_d.

Positive V is a replacement claim owed to the broker, not a broker market loss. A broker market loss over a move is old mark minus new mark. The finite-change identity retains the interaction of spot and foreign funding factors:

ΔV = q (D_f ΔS + S ΔD_f + ΔS ΔD_f - K ΔD_d)

Here the undifferenced S,D_f are the reference (old-state) values. There is no differentiation or discarded second-order term. A portfolio uses the existing finite dot-product aggregation; states can differ across trades to reflect their different remaining maturities. Phase controls which legs remain outstanding, but it is supplied rather than inferred from calendar dates. A mark change crossing settlement requires accounting for the settled cashflow separately.

The margin policy being proved

For one supplied client netting set, let Xᵢ be the signed broker replacement claim and Cᵢ eligible collateral in scenario i, in domestic units. Margin.agda defines

residualᵢ       = max(Xᵢ - Cᵢ, 0)
requiredMargin  = maxᵢ residualᵢ

This is the least nonnegative uniform domestic cash top-up covering every modeled scenario. Its defining guarantee is

requiredMargin ≤ b  ⇔  every Xᵢ ≤ Cᵢ + b        (provided b ≥ 0)

The implementation proves both directions, an equivalence of the certificate types, and their path obtained by univalence. It also proves monotonicity in claims, antitonicity in collateral, within-set netting/convexity bounds, and transport under a uniform claim error.

The guarantee is conditional on collateral actually being received, available, and realizable as modeled. postCash represents that changed state. A calculated or issued call does not itself change collateral. paid-required-margin-zero proves zero residual call after sufficient cash is posted. The general real-payment law margin-postCash identifies the remaining requirement as stopLoss(requiredMargin, paid) for any nonnegative real payment; the rational controller specializes this theorem. This is a specified stress-margin policy, not a claim about a particular jurisdiction's mandatory initial/variation-margin methodology.

Foreign collateral is valued in each scenario:

eligible collateral = domestic cash + retention × spot × foreign units

retention is the fraction remaining after a haircut. Valid input conditions include 0 ≤ retention ≤ 1 and nonnegative collateral amounts. The general spot-monotonicity theorem holds with claims fixed; FX swap claims that also move with spot must be revalued jointly, as in the example.

The arbitrary-scenario layer Margin/Scenario.agda expresses coverage for any supplied admissible state predicate. Its least nonnegative cash certificate covers both finite scenario lists and continuous uncertainty sets. An explicit binding state gives the stronger leastness statement for every real budget. The joint FX box example supplies those states instead of assuming a procedure that selects a maximizing real quote.

Two clients with matched FX swap portfolios

BrokerMargin.agda supplies two swap notionals, 60 and 40. Both have near/far rates 1 and dates 0/30. Client A holds one of each; client B holds their reversals. The example uses zero swap points to keep the arithmetic visible. The generic instrument permits different positive near and far rates. All amounts and shocks are illustrative model inputs.

The near leg has settled, and reference spot and both discount factors are 1, so initial marks are zero. Four valuation scenarios give:

Scenario Spot Domestic factor Foreign factor Broker claim on A Broker claim on B
Foreign appreciation 6/5 1 1 20 -20
Foreign depreciation 4/5 1 1 -20 20
Domestic factor falls 1 9/10 1 10 -10
Foreign factor falls 1 1 9/10 -10 10

The signed market marks sum to zero in every scenario. A and B are separate client netting sets, so the broker retains their separate positive claims. Client A has 5 domestic cash collateral. Client B has 10 foreign units with retention 4/5.

Scenario A collateral B eligible collateral A residual B residual Broker total residual
Foreign appreciation 5 48/5 15 0 15
Foreign depreciation 5 32/5 0 68/5 68/5
Domestic factor falls 5 8 5 0 5
Foreign factor falls 5 8 0 2 2

The exact cash calls are 15 for A and 68/5 (13.6) for B. Each is minimal for its own client and the four specified scenarios. After those amounts are received in domestic cash, their residual margin is zero. The separate calls total 143/5 (28.6), as proved by total-client-calls. Their binding scenarios differ. The broker's aggregate exposure maximum 15 and exposure CVaR 14.3 are different diagnostics and do not replace separately received collateral for each client under this margin policy.

If B's foreign collateral is frozen at its reference eligible value 8, the model asks for only 12. In the depreciation scenario, the claim has risen to 20 while eligible collateral has fallen to 32/5. Paying 12 then leaves 8/5 (1.6) uncovered. These values and the insufficiency proof are checked. This is adverse co-movement of claim and collateral values; no statistical default-correlation model is inferred from it.

For an additional distributional diagnostic, the four scenarios are assigned equal weights. Broker residual exposure has mean 89/10, maximum 15, and CVaR 143/10 at 50% confidence. An explicit threshold 5 and dual distribution (1/2,1/2,0,0) certify the CVaR value. These probabilities are illustrative market-scenario weights, not estimated probabilities of client default.

Joint market moves and an entire real uncertainty box

Single-factor tests do not by themselves bound simultaneous changes. FXBounds.agda proves interval bounds for every Cauchy-real state satisfying supplied box-membership evidence. For nonnegative notional:

q(S_low D_f_low - K D_d_high) ≤ V ≤ q(S_high D_f_high - K D_d_low)

Reversed swaps inherit the negated bounds. There is no case split on real order, grid search, or assumption that a general maximum exists.

JointFXMargin.agda applies this theory to the same client portfolios with spot in [4/5,6/5] and both discount factors in [9/10,1]. Every original scenario is in this box, but the box also admits joint moves.

Attaining joint state Spot Domestic factor Foreign factor Relevant claim Eligible collateral Required cash
A's maximum 6/5 9/10 1 30 5 25
B's maximum 4/5 1 9/10 28 32/5 108/5

The code proves both universal sufficiency and minimality:

25 ≤ cash_A       ⇔ A is covered at every real quote in the box
108/5 ≤ cash_B    ⇔ B is covered at every real quote in the box

B's claim maximum and collateral minimum occur at the same explicit state, so the combined bound is sharp. The reported requirements cover an entire real-valued uncertainty family, not only the two displayed witnesses. Box membership is an assumption on permitted valuation inputs, not a claim that the box contains every possible market state or imposes curve consistency.

Client Cash for four single-factor scenarios Cash for all quotes in the box Further cash after the old payment
A 15 25 10
B 68/5 108/5 8

The old payments are proved insufficient under the enlarged model. Exact rational reports on the two attaining states drive executable payment checks; their coverage of the whole box follows from the separate universal equivalence theorems. A generic ExactMargin report alone certifies only its specified finite scenario vectors.

Executable calls and received payments

MarginControl.agda uses an ExactMargin record containing a rational amount and proof that it equals the real scenario requirement. It does not assume every real requirement is rational. Given received nonnegative domestic cash, the further request is

outstanding = max(exact amount - cash already received, 0)

The request is proved minimal. It equals the residual margin recomputed after the actual payment. recordPayment returns a report for that changed collateral state; paying the further request clears the residual. checkPayment compares rationals and returns either coverage evidence or a proof that more cash is due.

BrokerOperations.agda checks these reductions for the four-scenario reports:

Operation Checked result
A pays 14 More due
A pays 15 Covered
B pays 12 More due; exact further request 8/5
B then pays 8/5 against its updated report Covered
B pays 68/5 at once Covered

After both clients pay the required cash, the broker residual and every admissible realized-closeout-loss severity are zero for these scenarios. The joint-box reports similarly reject the earlier payments 15 and 68/5. Changing the risk model requires a new certificate; a prior payment verdict does not automatically cover a larger uncertainty set.

Client identity, netting, and realized losses

ClientNetting.agda proves

max(Σ claims - Σ collateral, 0)
  ≤ Σ_clients max(claim_client - collateral_client, 0)

The left side is a pooled comparison quantity. It is not the separate-client exposure policy. The simple claims (10,-10) have total mark zero and positive exposure 10. In the broker example, pooling would incorrectly report zero residual despite the nonzero values above.

The HIT economic quotient therefore retains client and scenario labels. For fixed collateral, exposure descends through equality of all those claim profiles. A checked impossibility theorem shows that no separate-exposure function on aggregate-mark classes can preserve its value on every original position. If collateral also varies between positions, it must be included in the equivalence criterion. The supplied netting-set tags do not establish legal enforceability or prevent physical collateral from being double-counted by an external bookkeeping system.

CounterpartyRisk.agda distinguishes exposure from realized closeout loss. An input severity θⱼ ∈ [0,1] represents occurrence of default combined with the unrecovered fraction for client j:

realized closeout loss = Σⱼ θⱼ max(claimⱼ - collateralⱼ, 0)

The loss is nonnegative and bounded by broker residual exposure. The latter is the exact bound over every permitted severity, since all-one severity attains it. Severities may depend on both client and market scenario; no independence assumption is made. Worst-case, expectation, and CVaR acceptance certificates transfer from exposure to every such realized-loss profile. With every client claim covered, all these modeled closeout losses vanish. This does not remove a separate settlement-principal or liquidity obligation.

Funding and settlement remain separate controls

Settlement.agda keeps signed native-currency cashflows by trade and date. Outgoing principal is the positive part of the negative cashflow. It proves that algebraically netted outgoing amounts do not exceed gross outgoing amounts, but the inequality does not authorize payment netting. Funding certificates compare balances with outgoing amounts in each currency and at each date.

A single cashflow (-100 domestic, +100 foreign) has mark zero at spot 1, yet requires 100 domestic units. Holding only foreign currency does not satisfy that domestic funding certificate. A trade and its reversal have zero aggregate cashflow but gross outgoing payments of 100 in each currency. The generic converted principal-at-risk bounds retain these obligations.

The broker operations example constructs the actual four far-leg instructions from the 60/40 swaps and their reversals. It proves that every far date is 30, net cashflow is zero in both currencies, and gross outgoing amounts are (100 domestic,100 foreign). Those native-currency buffers satisfy its gross-funding certificate. At conversion factors (1,1), the gross amounts are worth 200 domestic units. Zero funding is proved insufficient even after the separate closeout margin calls have been paid; client collateral is not declared reusable for settlement funding.

A small delivery-state model distinguishes unreleased payments from irrevocably released principal, and pending/final/failed receipts. Under its explicit zero-recovery failure assumption, paid-away principal can be lost. An ideal atomic exchange condition proves this principal exposure and loss zero; it does not prove away replacement-cost, funding, or operational risk. The distinction between replacement costs, principal risk, and per-currency liquidity is motivated by the BIS FX settlement guidance. The formalization is a finite mathematical model, not an implementation or compliance assessment of an actual settlement system.

Each certificate applies to its specified stage and obligation. Combining closeout exposure, settlement principal, and liquidity into one capital number would require additional joint accounting.

Scope of the certificates

The proofs use constructive real order and lattice operations, cubical function extensionality, proposition-valued evidence, univalence, and HIT set quotients. No real-order decision procedure, missing-analysis postulate, or choice of a quotient representative is assumed.

Inputs still need a defensible valuation model, market shocks, collateral eligibility and realization assumptions, client grouping, and funding resources. The code does not estimate default probabilities, compute CVA, infer a legal netting right, perform a liquidation, or submit a margin call to a client. Calendar processing, minimum transfer amounts, rounding, settlement calendars, credit limits, custody and cash ledgers, and market-data integration are further application work.
