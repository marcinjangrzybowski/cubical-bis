Fees, model ambiguity, and sequential capital

The second development phase adds execution costs, optimization under uncertain probabilities, and two-stage risk evaluation. All values still live in the library's higher-inductive Cauchy reals. No additional analysis assumptions were needed. The examples answer different management questions; their risk criteria and budgets are stated separately below.

The README describes the shared four-scenario market and the original CVaR, stress, variance, and economic-quotient foundations.

The reference guide gives primary sources for proportional transaction costs, CVaR, robust optimization, and recursive multiple priors. The structure guide separates their reusable interfaces from the numerical examples.

The new TradingGuarantees supplies the large correctness statement for the fee-aware system: its success is equivalent to existence of an acceptable signed real fully invested portfolio. Its failure rules out every such competitor, while success returns an allocation with proved actual proportional fees, accepted all-in loss, and global optimality. See the motivating examples for the precise scope and comparison with the separate stress constraint.

An executable trading rule with fees

TransactionCosts.agda defines proportional costs by

cost(rates, old, target) = Σᵢ ratesᵢ |targetᵢ - oldᵢ|
allInLossᵢ               = cost + portfolioLossᵢ(target)

The generic proofs establish nonnegativity for nonnegative rates, zero cost for no trade, symmetry, the triangle inequality, monotonicity in rates, and convexity in target holdings from a fixed starting position. Adding the cost shifts mean loss, maximum loss, and certified CVaR by exactly that cost. Threshold/dual certificates and budget acceptance are transported explicitly. Convex combinations of targets have a proved bound on CVaR budgets including costs.

In Rebalancing.agda, the manager starts with all capital in asset A and chooses holdings (w, 1-w). Each asset has rate s/2, so charging both trade legs gives

fee(s,w) = s |1-w|
netLoss  = fee(s,w) + w A + (1-w) B

This equality is proved against the generic transaction-cost implementation. The nonnegative parameter s is an abstract fee per traded weight in the example's loss units, not a quoted percentage or a calibrated market rate.

For every Cauchy-real fee satisfying the indicated inequalities, the following global optima are proved. Competitors include every real weight and every real trial threshold, including signed holdings outside [0,1].

Fee regime Selected A/B weights Minimum CVaR including fees
0 ≤ s ≤ 4 3/4, 1/4 2 + s/4
4 ≤ s 1, 0 (no trade) 3

The proof combines two admissible dual lower bounds for raw CVaR with a supporting bound for the absolute-value fee. It does not require a complete piecewise evaluation of CVaR at every competing weight. At s=4 both stated choices attain 3; no uniqueness claim is made, including at s=0.

TradeDecision.agda turns this result into

chooseTrade : (s : ℚ) → 0 ℚ.≤ s → CertifiedTrade (rat s)

The returned record contains a real weight, proof that it is in [0,1], an attained CVaR value, and a lower-bound proof for every competitor. The chosenAllocation adapter returns the ordinary fully invested Allocation 2 used by the earlier portfolio layer. Rational comparison selects the fee regime; arbitrary real order is never decided.

The exact rational optimum also makes checkChosenBudget a complete decision procedure for rational budgets on this selected portfolio: it returns either acceptance or a proof of nonacceptance. Its domain requires nonnegative fees. Checked computational reductions include:

Fee s Selected A weight CVaR including fees Budget Decision
1 3/4 9/4 2 fail
1 3/4 9/4 9/4 pass
5 1 3 3 pass
4 3/4 3 3 pass

For fee 1, fully rebalancing to equal weights would instead have CVaR 5/2 and fails budget 9/4. The unconstrained selected trade optimizes CVaR alone: its raw maximum loss is 4, and its maximum loss including fees is 17/4. The original combined policy limits do not automatically hold for this trade.

Minimize CVaR while enforcing a stress cap

StressConstrainedTrade.agda adds an explicit constraint on the raw market loss: worstCase(L(w)) ≤ c. For every real cap 2 ≤ c ≤ 4 and fee s ≥ 0, it constructs the optimal allocation

A weight          = (c+2)/8
B weight          = (6-c)/8
raw scenario loss = (c, 4-c, 0, 0)
fee               = s(6-c)/8
minimum net CVaR  = 2 + s(6-c)/8
net stress        = c + s(6-c)/8

The raw stress constraint bounds any competitor's A weight by (c+2)/8. This forces a lower bound on its trading fee. Combining that bound with the universal raw-CVaR lower bound 2 proves the displayed constrained optimum. IsConstrainedOptimum bundles feasibility, an attained CVaR value, and the comparison against all feasible real weights and all real thresholds. capAllocation supplies the fully invested long-only holdings.

At fee 1 the cap gives a visible capital tradeoff:

Raw stress cap Optimal A weight Fee Minimum net CVaR Net stress
2 1/2 1/2 5/2 5/2
3 5/8 3/8 19/8 27/8
4 3/4 1/4 9/4 17/4

The middle row has dedicated checked numerical theorems. Budget 19/8 is accepted, while budget 9/4 is impossible for every portfolio meeting raw stress cap 3. The other rows are substitutions in the proved general formulas and agree with the earlier balanced and partial-trade values.

The initial all-A position has raw stress 6, so no trade is infeasible for all these caps. A large fee therefore does not remove the required trade when the stress cap is mandatory. Net stress is reported separately: it includes fees and exceeds the raw cap whenever the fee is positive.

Allocation under uncertain scenario probabilities

Ambiguity.agda takes a nonempty finite family of real distributions and defines risk as the maximum of their expected losses. The expectation of any real convex mixture equals the corresponding mixture of model expectations. Consequently, bounding all listed models is equivalent to bounding all their mixtures. An additional formulation covers arbitrary finite selections of the original labels, including repetitions. Dirac weights prove the converse without selecting a maximizing real value.

The resulting risk satisfies monotonicity, cash translation, nonnegative homogeneity, subadditivity, weighted bounds, and uniform error bounds. This is an ambiguous expected-loss criterion, distinct from CVaR at a specified confidence level.

RobustAllocation.agda keeps the original asset losses but uses two candidate probability laws:

Model A stress B stress Neutral 1 Neutral 2
Up 1/2 1/4 1/4 0
Down 1/8 1/2 3/8 0

For L(w) = w A + (1-w) B, their expectations are

E_Up[L(w)]   = 1/2 + 2w
E_Down[L(w)] = 11/4 - 3w

A fixed adversarial mixture with model weights (3/5,2/5) has expectation 7/5 at every real weight. It supplies a universal lower bound. At w=9/20, both models attain 7/5, proving global optimality. The two affine inequalities also prove uniqueness: any real weight meeting capital 7/5 equals 9/20. The selected weight is within the long-only interval.

Quantity Equal weights Robustly selected A weight 9/20
Worst expected loss across models/mixtures 3/2 7/5
Maximum scenario loss 2 12/5
Mean under the old uniform law 1 1

The strict improvement in ambiguous expected loss and the strict deterioration in stress loss are both proved. Capital 7/5 rejects equal weights under the ambiguity criterion. This example has no transaction fees; selecting a portfolio jointly under costs, ambiguity, and all policy limits requires a further constrained model.

Two-stage risk and local capital certificates

DynamicRisk.agda defines CoherentRisk using monotonicity, cash translation, nonnegative homogeneity, and subadditivity. Normalization and evaluation of constants follow from those laws. Expectation, worst-case loss, and finite model ambiguity provide constructed instances.

A LossTree m n has a first observed branch and then a terminal leaf. With an outer risk rule and one inner rule per branch, backward evaluation is

continuationRisk(X)ᵢ = innerᵢ(Xᵢ)
rootRisk(X)         = outer(continuationRisk(X))

The composition inherits the risk laws. If every revised continuation risk is at most its previous value, root risk is at most its previous value. This is the proved two-stage time-consistency property. It allows changes which do not improve loss at every terminal leaf.

In the checked DownstreamControl example, the outer rule is maximum and each inner rule is fair expectation:

Branch Original leaf losses Revised leaf losses Conditional risk before/after
0 (8,-2) (2,0) 3 → 1
1 (0,2) (0,2) 1 → 1

Root capital falls from 3 to 1 even though the gain -2 at one leaf becomes loss 0. This is a comparison of supplied loss trees, not a proof that a particular trading strategy can implement the revision.

A NodeBudgetCertificate records intermediate branch budgets and their aggregate root bound. It implies root acceptance, and exact continuation risks construct a certificate from any root bound. Propositional truncation of such certificates is equivalent to root acceptance. Known branch costs can be added after evaluating future local risk; nonuniform error reserves propagate through the outer rule. Nested maxima are characterized by their leafwise bounds and commute when the two finite indices are exchanged. For expectation, multiplying initial and conditional probabilities gives total mass one and a proved finite tower formula, including zero atoms.

How independent branch uncertainty changes capital

DynamicAmbiguity.agda composes finite ambiguous expectations. It proves that root capital bounds expected loss under every outer mixture and every independently chosen family of inner mixtures, with arbitrary real probability weights on the supplied model lists. Node-budget certificates have the same soundness guarantee. The generic theorem is an upper bound; a converse or exact supremum representation for all rectangular models is not claimed.

The explicit RectangularGap example has fair initial branches and losses (4,0) at each branch. Two coupled global models select opposite leaves:

Global model Selected leaf at branch 0 Selected leaf at branch 1 Expected loss
Model 0 0 1 2
Model 1 1 0 2

Every mixture of these coupled models meets budget 2. If conditional model choices are allowed independently after each branch, both branches may select leaf 0, and nested risk is exactly 4. The strict gap and the impossibility of a rectangular budget of 2 are checked. Enlarging the uncertainty set changes the capital requirement; nesting does not preserve the original coupled model's risk value.

The mathematical background for nested risk and time consistency includes Ruszczyński's Risk-Averse Dynamic Programming for Markov Decision Processes. The distinction between coupled uncertainty and dynamic decomposition is discussed in Shapiro's Rectangular sets of probability measures. These are conceptual references. The Agda development proves its finite statements locally and does not import the papers' analytic or optimization existence results.

A precise limit on economic netting

ExecutionEquivalence.agda applies the existing HIT economic quotient to total losses including fees. For a fixed market, rate vector, initial holding, and nonempty scenario set, it proves:

given equal raw loss profiles,
equal execution classes ≃ equal transaction costs

Fees can therefore be carried from raw economic classes to execution classes exactly when they are invariant under raw loss equivalence. Sufficiency uses the set-quotient recursor; necessity uses quotient effectiveness and cancellation at one scenario. The relevant equalities and equivalences ignore alternative proofs of the same financial condition.

The execution example shows that the direct equal-weight holding and the wrapped basket still have the same raw economic class. Starting at zero, with asset rates (1,1,3), their fees are respectively 1 and 3. Their CVaRs including fees are 3 and 5: at budget 3, direct execution passes and basket execution fails. Their execution classes are unequal, and no map from the raw quotient to the execution quotient can agree with execution on every representative.

This does not exclude arbitrary functions between the quotient types. Nor does a total-loss class retain enough state for future rebalancing: actual holdings may still be needed to calculate later fees.

Foundation and scope

All probabilities, weights, fees, and risk values in the general statements are Cauchy reals. Real maxima and absolute values use constructive lattice operations. Supporting inequalities avoid real-order case splits; the executable trade and budget decisions compare rationals. Cubical function extensionality, effective HIT set quotients, propositional truncation, and univalence connect financial representations and policy evidence. No choice of a maximizing model or a representative of a quotient is required.

Fees are deterministic additional losses with prescribed target holdings. Funding feasibility is outside this model: fees are not deducted from the target notionals, and a cash account is not constructed. Rates remain fixed for the triangle and convexity theorems. Prices, interest, slippage, liquidity, fixed ticket charges, and market-data calibration are absent.

The dynamic layer has a fixed finite two-stage tree. It does not construct a filtration, recover conditional probabilities from a joint law, or solve a general dynamic trading problem. A generic static CVaR risk function is not assumed to exist or to be time consistent. Every numerical optimum used here has an explicit checked proof.
