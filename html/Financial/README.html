Constructive portfolio risk over Cauchy reals

This development implements a finite-scenario portfolio risk system in safe Cubical Agda. Its inputs may be arbitrary higher-inductive Cauchy reals, not just embedded rational numbers. The application supplies exact rational data and proves a portfolio optimum, executes a certified budget check, and carries policy evidence through rebalancing and economic netting. Extensions add transaction costs, executable optimal trade selection, probability ambiguity, and sequential capital control. The instrument extension adds deliverable FX swaps and broker margin, including foreign collateral, client netting sets, default/recovery severities, and currency-specific settlement funding.

For a presentation to quantitative developers, start with Broker2: one engine, seven versions. Each version evolves quoteCall, receive, and its correctness contract, with a proved connection to the preceding version. The 28-minute speaker guide follows the program from one cash account to replay-safe receipt processing and certified revaluation, without introducing concrete market scenarios.

Its final model is implemented by the relational pipeline. Typed trade and receipt tables feed projection, selection and grouped sums. The implementation returns ordinary data; separate proofs connect those outputs to the abstract engine and its broker-wide financial guarantee. Start with the single margin-report calculation: its source reads and relational stages appear together in calculate. Computation and certification are organized in Implementation/ and Properties/.

For the generic broker machinery, start with the new Broker correctness progression: eight stages from a single cash account through scenario reports, separate netting sets, inventory-backed collateral, identified receipts, revaluation and minimum transfers to a composed controller. Its final theorem bounds every client and aggregate modeled loss after actual receipts, with zero loss at zero transfer tolerance. The progression contains no concrete scenarios; its references explain the literature basis and its progress log records checks.

Start with System correctness: motivating examples. The flagship broker guarantee proves that an executable payment/funding check succeeds exactly when every client is covered at every real quote in the specified box and the actual settlement book has enough native-currency funding. Success yields zero modeled closeout loss for every admissible default/recovery realization. The same statement holds for arbitrary finite histories of actual client receipts.

The trading guarantee turns the fee-aware optimizer into a complete feasibility solver: success returns a certified allocation, while failure proves that no signed real fully invested competitor meets the stated tail budget in this market.

FX_BROKER.md and EXTENSIONS.md explain the component models. Application.agda and Diversification.agda provide the original portfolio workflow and optimizer. PROGRESS.md records implementation milestones, independent reviews, and validation.

FX instruments and broker margin

The FX broker guide develops two-leg deliverable FX swaps, exact spot/discount-factor valuation changes, and the least domestic cash top-up covering a client's stressed replacement claims. Currency collateral is revalued with the claims; a calculated call becomes protective collateral only after it is paid and available as modeled.

Two clients hold opposite portfolios of swaps with notionals 60 and 40. Their market marks cancel, but client identity and principal obligations remain relevant:

Control Checked result
Four single-factor scenarios Client cash calls 15 and 68/5; frozen foreign collateral leaves a proved 8/5 shortfall.
All real quotes in a joint spot/discount box Sharp cash calls 25 and 108/5; earlier payments require a further 10 and 8.
Received payments Arbitrary receipt histories preserve exact outstanding calls and whole-box coverage; equal per-client totals preserve the combined broker decision.
Counterparty loss Exposure bounds every supplied default/recovery severity; paid sufficient collateral eliminates modeled residual closeout loss.
Settlement funding Actual far legs net to zero but require gross native-currency payments of 100 domestic and 100 foreign; the combined checker rejects 200 domestic and zero foreign.
End-to-end broker policy Pass iff all-client whole-box coverage and gross funding hold; staged receipts yield a certificate for the actual swap book.

Client-labelled HIT quotients preserve exposure; an aggregate-mark quotient is proved to lose information needed for that calculation. The box results cover an infinite family of Cauchy-real states using constructive bounds and explicit attaining witnesses. The guide distinguishes these mathematical guarantees from valuation calibration, legal enforceability, and operational receipt of collateral.

Model and conventions

A loss vector is Fin n → ℝ. Positive values are losses; negative values are gains. A distribution contains nonnegative real scenario weights and a proof that their finite sum is one. Zero-probability scenarios are permitted.

A market maps each asset to a scenario-loss vector. Portfolio losses are the finite dot product of holdings and unit asset losses. General holdings can be negative; the Allocation subtype restricts them to nonnegative weights with total capital one. Amounts in the diversification example are abstract loss units over a single horizon, not prices or percentages inferred from market data.

The model separates three controls:

Control Meaning
Expected loss Probability-weighted mean loss
Worst-case stress loss Maximum over a nonempty scenario set, including zero-probability stress scenarios
Expected shortfall / CVaR Attained minimum of the threshold objective described below

Variance and covariance provide additional dispersion diagnostics; variance is distinct from a loss-based capital requirement. The extensions also study the worst expected loss across a finite family of probability models and its entire convex hull, and compose risk rules on a two-stage scenario tree.

Further theory and applications

The extension guide gives definitions, proof ideas, precise input assumptions, and the following checked results:

Question Result
When should a concentrated portfolio trade, including proportional fees? For fee parameter 0 ≤ s ≤ 4, weights (3/4,1/4) attain CVaR 2+s/4; for s ≥ 4, no trade attains the minimum 3. Rational inputs give executable certified trade and budget decisions.
What if the trade must also obey a scenario stress cap? For 2 ≤ c ≤ 4, A weight (c+2)/8 minimizes CVaR including any nonnegative proportional fee subject to raw stress at most c. Fee 1 and cap 3 give CVaR 19/8; budget 9/4 is impossible for every feasible portfolio.
What if scenario probabilities are uncertain? A different allocation (9/20,11/20) uniquely minimizes worst expected loss across two models and all their mixtures, with capital 7/5. Its scenario stress loss increases from 2 to 12/5.
Can managers check capital locally at intermediate branches? Two-stage coherent risk composes with proved time consistency, node-budget propagation, and a finite expectation tower formula.
Does independently changing models at each branch matter? Coupled uncertainty has capital 2 in a checked example; independent branch uncertainty gives nested capital 4.
Can equal raw portfolios still have different execution risk? A direct basket and a wrapped basket have identical raw losses but fees 1 and 3, producing CVaR 3 and 5. Quotient descent is characterized by fee invariance.

The trading and ambiguity examples optimize different criteria. The stress-constrained trade explicitly couples a raw scenario limit with CVaR including fees; the other optima do not assert automatic compliance with the original combined policy limits.

Worked diversification and optimization

Each scenario has probability 1/4.

Scenario Asset A Asset B Equal-weight portfolio
A stress 6 -2 2
B stress -2 6 2
Neutral 1 0 0 0
Neutral 2 0 0 0

The tail multiplier is κ = 2, corresponding to the worst 50% of probability mass. This small confidence level makes every step of the example visible.

Quantity Asset A Asset B Equal-weight portfolio
Expected loss 1 1 1
Worst-case stress loss 6 6 2
CVaR at 50% confidence 3 3 2
Variance 9 9 1

For every real weight w, including shorted allocations, let L(w) = w A + (1-w) B. The distribution (1/2, 1/2, 0, 0) is an admissible dual witness with expected loss exactly 2 for every w. Weak duality proves 2 ≤ F(L(w), t) for every real threshold t. The equal-weight allocation attains 2 at threshold 0, so it is a global optimizer over both variables. This result does not depend on a grid search or an unproved optimizer.

CVaR minimizers need not be unique. Worst-case stress risk provides a stronger selection criterion in this example: the two stress losses are 8w-2 and 6-8w, and requiring both to be at most 2 forces w = 1/2. The quarter allocation is a checked second CVaR optimizer: it has losses (0,4,0,0), CVaR 2, stress loss 4, and variance 3.

VarianceExample.agda proves covariance -7 between the assets and the identity

variance(L(w)) = 1 + 32 (w - 1/2)²

for every real weight. Nonnegativity of real squares proves global variance minimization at equal weights without assuming independent asset returns.

Expected-shortfall certificates

For a distribution P and a real multiplier κ ≥ 1, define

positivePart(x) = max(x, 0)
stopLoss(x, t)  = positivePart(x - t)
F(L, t)        = t + κ E_P[stopLoss(L, t)]

The usual confidence parameter is related by κ = 1/(1-confidence). The formal input is κ and its lower-bound proof, avoiding a partial inverse. This threshold formulation follows Rockafellar and Uryasev's treatment of general loss distributions, including atoms. It does not identify CVaR with naive conditioning on a strict exceedance of a quantile; that would mishandle discrete mass at a threshold. See their 2002 paper.

An admissible dual distribution Q satisfies Q_i ≤ κ P_i for every scenario. The implementation proves

E_Q[L] ≤ F(L, t)

directly, for every threshold. A concrete OptimalityCertificate supplies a threshold, an admissible dual distribution, and equality of these two bounds. The resulting value is a global minimum and independent of the certificate. The nominal distribution is itself proved admissible, so the dual family is inhabited and every threshold objective is bounded below by the mean loss. A pointwise scenario bound supplies an upper certificate.

IsCVaR L r expresses the global minimum value and propositionally truncated attainment. There is at most one such real value. General existence of an optimizer for every real-valued finite dataset is not asserted here; the worked examples construct their optimizers in full. No missing analysis result is postulated or silently used.

RiskLaws.agda proves monotonicity, cash translation, subadditivity, weighted convex bounds, and uniform perturbation bounds for values supplied with IsCVaR evidence. It also proves that acceptance is equivalent to r ≤ budget, and gives the corresponding univalence path.

Executable budget control

RiskControl.Report contains a trial threshold, an admissible dual measure, and verified rational bounds enclosing their objective values. check uses decidable rational comparisons:

  • If the upper bound is within the budget, return acceptance with a proof.
  • If the budget is strictly below the lower bound, return rejection with a proof that no threshold can certify the budget.
  • Otherwise return inconclusive.

Inconclusiveness can reflect coarse numerical bounds or a gap between the chosen primal and dual witnesses. It is not an assertion that the portfolio violates its budget. The four reduction checks in Application.agda establish:

Input Budget Result
Equal-weight portfolio, exact report [2,2] 2 pass
Equal-weight portfolio, exact report [2,2] 1 fail
Concentrated asset A, exact report [3,3] 2 fail
Equal-weight portfolio, coarse report [1,3] 2 unknown

These labels carry logical evidence via check-sound. The checker never branches on equality, sign, or order of arbitrary Cauchy reals.

Rebalancing, reserves, and economic identity

Policy combines a mean-loss limit, a stress limit, and a tail-risk limit. It proves that a convex blend of two compliant portfolios satisfies the same limits. rebalance-certified returns both the new fully invested allocation and its policy evidence for every real weight in [0,1].

For nonnegative holdings, asset-specific one-sided model error bounds combine into the dot product of holdings and errors. Adding that reserve transports a tail certificate to the actual model. Moving the threshold with a uniform loss error gives coefficient one on the reserve, rather than κ.

The application adds a third instrument which wraps the equal-weight basket. The allocations (1/2,1/2,0) and (0,0,1) have different holdings but identical losses. Their proof-bearing policy records become equal in the economic quotient. This netting result concerns scenario losses; operational details such as transaction fees must be included in those losses to be covered.

HoTT and Cubical Agda content

  • The real numbers are the library's higher-inductive Cauchy reals. Constructive lattice maxima and stop losses work without decidable real order.
  • Positive parts and fixed-threshold stop losses preserve the native Cauchy closeness relation through the existing HIT lifting of maximum.
  • Cubical function extensionality turns pointwise loss equality into paths.
  • A HIT set quotient identifies economically equivalent positions. Its equality is proved equivalent to pointwise loss equality, and its loss map is an embedding.
  • The quotient is equivalent to the image with propositionally truncated fibers of the loss map. No representative selection or choice axiom is needed. Univalence gives a path between the two type presentations, with risk-preservation theorems for the equivalence.
  • Order proofs and policy evidence are propositions. Two proofs do not create two different certified economic positions.
  • Truncation hides threshold witnesses in acceptance and attainment while retaining all proposition-valued consequences.

The real rebalancing segment is not a cubical equality path between unequal real numbers. Cauchy reals and the relevant portfolio types are sets; the quotient and proof-transport techniques provide the pertinent HoTT structure.

Scope and extension points

This is a checked finite mathematical system, with executable example checks. It does not include market-data ingestion, floating-point numerical solvers, a general CVaR minimizer, continuous-time trading, or probability calibration. The supplied probabilities and error certificates are model inputs; proofs establish consequences of those inputs, not their empirical accuracy.

Useful extensions are a verified numerical routine that produces Report bounds, certificates generated by a rational linear-programming solver, an explicit funding account, and scenario-label invariance. Any optimizer used externally can remain outside the trust boundary: Agda can check its threshold and dual witness with the existing certificate interface.

Transaction costs are now formalized as additional losses with prescribed target holdings. Self-financing execution and probability calibration remain outside the model. The dynamic layer is a finite two-stage evaluation and certificate system, without a general dynamic trading optimizer.

The entire development stays under this example directory and its umbrella module. It uses the project's --safe --cubical settings, with no new postulates, unsafe pragmas, holes, or changes to foundational APIs.
