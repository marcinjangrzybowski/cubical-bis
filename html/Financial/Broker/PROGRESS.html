Broker progression: implementation and validation

8 September 2026 — generic machinery, eight stages

The requested separate folder now contains eight modules and 1,482 Agda source lines. The progression extracts the important lessons of the earlier FX broker example into generic machinery: client identity, sufficient versus exact margin, actual receipt versus requested cash, collateral valuation, and composition of whole-book guarantees. It introduces no concrete scenarios or calibrated data.

The reading guide describes each stage's inputs and results; REFERENCES.md connects the methods to six primary source groups. The public Broker.agda entry point exports all stages, and the reusable Theory.agda exposes them under Broker.

Work completed

  1. Cash accounts: least nonnegative calls, receipt composition, exact remaining calls, and a univalence path between payment adequacy and coverage.
  2. Scenario reports: arbitrary admissible domains; sufficient requirements and rational lower/upper enclosures. Assessments distinguish proven coverage, proven deficiency, and uncertainty. Exact enclosures give complete decisions.
  3. Client netting: arbitrary finite books, per-client checks, total necessary cash lower bounds, and full-coverage/zero-loss certificates for all severities.
  4. Collateral: inventory-backed allocations, scenario-dependent prices and haircut retention, total eligible-value bounds, and coverage-preserving substitutions, including changed asset counts.
  5. Receipt journal: finite immutable receipt catalog, owner attribution, duplicate suppression, batching/reordering paths, and exact incremental cash accounting. Batches may have arbitrary length and repeated IDs.
  6. Revaluation: conservative claim-rise/collateral-fall reserves, exposure and loss bounds before reserve payment, coverage after receipt, and composition of consecutive drift bounds.
  7. Minimum transfers: an executable full-gap-or-zero call policy; actual requested payments give residual bounded by MTA. Zero MTA gives full coverage.
  8. Controller: old upper requirements plus certified rational drift reserves drive calls on current journal cash. A rational gate checks new receipts per client and returns a system certificate for the actual updated ledger. All client residuals and aggregate severity-weighted loss are bounded; zero MTA yields a whole-book zero-loss guarantee. Inventory plus extra cash bounds eligible value and controlled positive claims. Cubical paths identify replayed ledgers together with independently reconstructed certificates.

Independent reviews

Three agents developed collateral, identified receipts, revaluation, and transfer policy alongside the root's account, report, netting, and controller work. Reviews cross-checked the interfaces and the final composition. No mathematical defect remained after the normal compiler edit/check loops.

The reviews singled out conditions now stated in both code and the guide:

  • A sufficient requirement describes a hypothetical protective balance; it does not prove that balance has already been received.
  • The final gate compares requests with newly credited per-client receipts. Replaying previously credited IDs cannot satisfy another positive request.
  • Journal cash is additional to base collateral inventory; asset pools and receipt IDs must represent distinct resources in the supplied model.
  • A failed conservative plan is not necessarily an unsafe account. Only the exact-report assessment has a complete economic coverage decision.
  • MTA gives a nonzero residual allowance; zero modeled loss needs zero MTA or separately proved complete coverage.
  • resources includes unallocated inventory and gives a valuation bound, not a liquidity, solvency, custody, or legal-enforceability certificate.
  • The complete certified-state replay path keeps catalog, valuation, claims, admissibility, policy, and MTA fixed. Withdrawals and reversals need new theory.
