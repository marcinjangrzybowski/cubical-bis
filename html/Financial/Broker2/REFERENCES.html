Sources and presentation choices

This progression formalizes a small margin engine and its successive changes. The financial sources motivate the concepts; the version-to-version refinements, rational report updates and Cubical certificate paths are theorems of this implementation. No numerical scenario or parameter calibration is supplied.

Versions Source What it motivates
V01–V03 BCBS, Supervisory guidance for managing risks associated with the settlement of foreign exchange transactions (2013), §§3.3.5–3.3.8. Official guidance. Replacement-cost exposure, separate authorized netting sets, and collateral availability/valuation.
V03–V04 A. Ben-Tal and A. Nemirovski, Robust Convex Optimization, Mathematics of Operations Research 23(4), 769–805 (1998). Author-hosted paper. Interpreting coverage as a constraint that holds for every member of a supplied uncertainty set.
V05 BCBS–IOSCO, Margin requirements for non-centrally cleared derivatives, April 2020 revision, requirement 2.3 and Element 4. Dated standard. Minimum transfer amounts and conservative collateral concepts. The precise full-gap-or-zero policy and equality boundary are explicit model choices.

V06 J. H. Saltzer, D. P. Reed, and D. D. Clark, End-to-End Arguments in System Design, ACM TOCS 2(4), 277–288 (1984). MIT-hosted text. Duplicate suppression using knowledge at the application boundary.
V07 D. Brigo and A. Pallavicini, Nonlinear consistent valuation of CCP cleared or CSA bilateral trades with initial margins under credit, funding and wrong-way risks, Journal of Financial Engineering 1(1), 1450001 (2014). Publisher DOI. Explicitly accounting for collateral and adverse changes before cash protection is available. Our reserve is a deterministic bound, not that paper's stochastic valuation model.

The earlier broker bibliography supplies fuller bibliographic details, section references and source-specific limitations. The presentation uses these concepts to explain software contracts rather than to reproduce the cited methodologies in full.

Limits of attribution

The rational report interface is our constructive bridge from real risk to executable amount decisions. For arbitrary real profiles it consumes a certified bound. For rational profiles RationalInputs computes and proves exact finite maxima. Neither behavior is attributed to a cited optimization algorithm.

The MTA theorem bounds a residual allowance after actual receipts. Physically settled FX forwards/swaps are excluded from the 2020 standard's general margin requirements; its requirement 1.1 points to separate variation-margin guidance and national treatment. No assertion of regulatory applicability or compliance is made for this generic code.

The event catalog fixes each owner and amount. Distinct IDs must denote distinct actual receipts, separate from base collateral. The CDM and duplicate-suppression references motivate that distinction; they do not authenticate our inputs or establish durable, concurrent banking operations.

The final guarantee concerns modeled positive unsecured replacement exposure and supplied severities in [0,1]. Settlement principal, funding liquidity, liquidation and model adequacy require separate contracts. The proof assistant checks consequences of the supplied data and evidence; it does not certify that a chosen stress family describes the future market.

Relational pipeline: representation, identity, and accounting

The pipeline guide starts with the standalone Implementation/Calculation function. Its calculate body chains typed source rows through deriveᵣ, selectᵣ, grouping, a maximum fold, and projection. It returns the current call table. The Implementation/ modules contain ordinary calculations; the separate Properties/ modules contain their accounting and financial proofs.

Properties/Calculation proves calculate-refines-model for the actual output table, using the current exact initial report and V06 quote. calculate-certified certifies every returned row; its upper-exact field connects the amount to the independent indexed referenceBook. fulfilling-calculation then gives the tolerance guarantee when additional nonnegative actual payments meet that table's requests. The supplied valuation functions are shared by implementation and specification; accounting refinement is not independent pricing validation.

The optional Margin engine retains the broader receipt/revaluation lifecycle and its complete output refinement. Its maintained conservative quotes need not equal the standalone calculation's recomputed exact current quote. The computed-report paths do not identify arbitrary conservative reports. These are implementation theorems, not results attributed to the sources below. Existing and additional receipt cash is supplied as available at par in the common account currency; the literature does not establish that premise for an input catalog.

Pipeline concept Primary source and scope
Separating a logical data model from a machine representation Codd (1970) motivates the separation; our exact accounting and model-refinement paths are proved in Agda.
Grouping positions into an authorized netting set and valuing available collateral BCBS FX guidance, especially §§3.3.5–3.3.8. A pipeline client key must denote a permissible netting group; the source does not certify the adapter's grouping.
Business receipt identity independent of message delivery FINOS CDM. External labels are metadata in our schema; immutable internal keys drive actual receipt accounting.
Suppressing replay at the application boundary Saltzer, Reed, and Clark. The implementation proves ledger and balance identities, without a durable-storage or authentication theorem.
Uniform protection across supplied scenarios Ben-Tal and Nemirovski motivates requiring coverage for every supplied scenario. The exact finite calculation and its row certificate are proved here.
Adverse revaluation in the optional broader engine Brigo and Pallavicini motivates explicit margining/gap risk. Summing positive scenario changes is our sufficient reserve, not a calibrated initial-margin method or a least-reserve claim.

Completeness, business-key uniqueness, custody availability, sufficient instrument identifiers, and snapshot consistency remain source-adapter conditions. The formal refinements certify consequences of those supplied data. They do not establish that the catalog describes the external business correctly.

Codd: logical data representation, 1970

E. F. Codd, A relational model of data for large shared data banks, Communications of the ACM 13(6), 377–387, 1 June 1970. Publisher DOI: 10.1145/362384.362685, IBM Research publication record.

Codd motivates protecting applications from changes in internal data representation and introduces a relational model. This provides context for separating pipeline row operations from their indexed financial meaning. Publisher abstract.

Our Relation' is a list with explicit order and multiplicity. The pipeline implements specified projection, selection, and aggregation functions over it; it does not claim all relational-algebra or SQL laws, query-optimizer correctness, database normalization, or transaction isolation. The refinement proofs and Cauchy-real risk guarantees are this implementation's results.
