One broker margin engine, evolved seven times

Broker2 is a presentation development for quantitative developers: start with one account, add business requirements, and keep proving what the same engine does. The recurring interface is quoteCall, receive, receive-correct. Every successor imports the preceding version and states its compatibility contract explicitly.

Read the modules in order. Use PRESENTATION.md for a 28-minute speaker guide, REFERENCES.md for the financial and systems motivation, and PROGRESS.md for reviews and validation. The entry point checks all seven versions and the relational implementation together.

The final version also serves as the abstract model for Pipeline/. It materializes typed trade and collateral relations, values and groups them by client netting set, computes margin reserves, and processes identified receipt deliveries. Its pipeline-correct theorem connects the returned state, dependent report, and call table to V07 and carries the same financial guarantee to independent sums over the source catalogs. This adds a concrete implementation to the presentation without introducing a numerical scenario. The executable modules return ordinary data. Separate property modules certify those outputs; field access and aggregation use shared relation primitives.

For a compact demonstration, start with Implementation/Calculation.agda. Its calculate function reads trades, custody lots and recorded receipts, chains relational enrichment and aggregation, and returns a margin-call table. Properties/Calculation.agda certifies that table against the model. Implementation/ and Properties/ keep the calculation and its certification in separate folders; demonstrating this query does not require walking through the complete engine workflow.

The evolution

Version Change to the engine Result proved Relationship to the preceding version
V01 Account A signed claim, available cash, and an additional cash call. A sufficient receipt covers the claim; the quote is the least such nonnegative receipt. Baseline. Two successive receipts equal one combined receipt.
V02 Clients Lift the same state and operations to separate client netting sets. Every client is covered; aggregate residual and all modeled severity-weighted losses are zero. Projecting a client commutes with both operations: quote-refines-V01, receive-refines-V01.
V03 Stress A market profile and common received cash; one call must work across all supplied stresses. The least nonnegative uniform call covers every client in every supplied scenario. Receipt and scenario projection commute. A singleton market recovers V02's quote: receive-refines-V02, singleton-quote-refines-V02.
V04 Rational A rational report on the real quote, a decidable receipt gate, and an updated report after payment. Passing the quoted plan proves coverage. Exact reports make the decision complete for coverage. Rational inputs compute exact initial reports. Rational receipts embed into the V03 transition: receive-refines-V03. Reports bound the V03 quote; equality is an additional proved property.
V05 Tolerance Suppress calls at or below each client's minimum transfer amount. Sufficient actual receipts leave residual at most MTA per client; aggregate residual and every severity-weighted loss are at most total MTA. Receipt behavior is inherited; zero MTA gives exactly V04's quote: zero-mta-refines-V04.
V06 Receipts Replace amount-only receipt input with a market and identified-event ledger. Return the next state, certified report, and assessment. The same loss bounds hold using newly credited cash; replay and order preserve the resulting state. receive-refines-V05 identifies the projected transition with V05 receiving precisely the new cash.
V07 Revaluation Preserve the receipt ledger, change the market, refresh the report with a certified reserve, and run the same engine. The final receipt operation bounds all client residuals and aggregate losses under the new market; zero MTA gives full coverage and zero modeled loss. An unchanged market and zero reserve recover V06: quote-refines-V06, receive-refines-V06.

This is a sequence of engine versions. Earlier states and operations remain visible through projections or specializations. The table does not claim that every version has the same state type, that every later quote equals an earlier one, or that finite-catalog cash represents every arbitrary real balance. The named theorems give the precise relationship in each case.

The contract grows with the program

V01 starts with the familiar formula

quoteCall(b) = max(claim(b) − cash(b), 0)

and a theorem whose conclusion mentions the actual state transition:

receive-correct : ∀ b paid paid₊ →
  quoteCall b ≤ᵣ paid → Safe (receive b paid paid₊)

paid₊ proves that the received amount is nonnegative. This is a proof for every input of the specified type, rather than a claim about selected evaluated cases. A call calculation does not record a receipt.

V02 adds a client quantifier. V03 adds a scenario quantifier. V04 connects the real specification to a rational executable gate. V05 changes the postcondition from zero residual to an explicit tolerance. V06 changes the representation of receipts and proves a commuting state transition. V07 changes the market inputs and proves that a refreshed report suffices. The program and its contract evolve together.

In the final version, let:

  • X₁(s,i) be the new signed claim for scenario s and netting set i;
  • C₁(s,i) be its eligible collateral, excluding separately recorded cash;
  • p′(i) be cash derived from the updated receipt ledger;
  • t(i) ≥ 0 be that netting set's MTA;
  • θ(i) be any supplied severity between zero and one.

If the newly credited receipts meet every computed request, receive-correct constructs a Guarantee of

max(X₁(s,i) − (C₁(s,i) + p′(i)), 0) ≤ t(i)                for every s, i
Σᵢ max(X₁(s,i) − (C₁(s,i) + p′(i)), 0) ≤ Σᵢ t(i)          for every s
Σᵢ θ(i) max(X₁(s,i) − (C₁(s,i) + p′(i)), 0) ≤ Σᵢ t(i)     for every s, θ

zero-mta-safe and zero-mta-loss-zero specialize this to full coverage and zero modeled loss. Client identity is retained before taking positive parts; a different client's surplus cannot discharge an unsecured claim.

The client count, scenario count, real market values, receipt catalog size, and batch length are parameters. There is at least one supplied scenario; clients or events may be empty. These are mathematical quantifiers, not a production throughput benchmark. The theorem covers the supplied finite stress family; it does not assert that future markets stay inside it.

A complete report and receipt cycle

The report convention is deliberately stable throughout V04–V07:

Report b bounds the current INCREMENTAL requirement at state b.
Existing cash is already accounted for inside b.

For arbitrary Cauchy-real inputs, a caller supplies a nonnegative rational upper bound with its proof. For rational claim/collateral profiles and existing cash, V04Rational.RationalInputs computes initialState, initialAmounts, and initialReport; initialReport-exact proves those computed calls equal the real specification. No sample market or assumed optimizer is needed.

After receiving new cash d, advanceReport calculates

next upper = max(current upper − d, 0)

and proves that it bounds the new state's requirement. V04 also proves exactness is preserved when the old report was exact. Partial payments produce valid next reports; no new analysis certificate is needed for that receipt update.

V06 derives d from newlyPaid(oldLedger, batch). IDs already credited and repetitions within the batch contribute no additional cash. Its process returns a Processed record with:

Field Meaning
next-state The actual computed market-and-ledger state after ingesting the batch.
state-correct A path identifying that returned state with receive s batch.
next-report A report indexed by the returned state, ready for the next call.
assessment Either evidence that the new receipts fulfilled the quote and produced the risk guarantee, or a client whose quote remains unmet.

A failed assessment does not roll back an actual receipt and does not assert that the account is unsafe: a conservative report can overstate the true requirement. This differs from V04's complete economic decision when exactness is supplied.

For revaluation, Update s contains a new market and uniform nonnegative bounds on claim rises and effective-collateral falls. The ledger is unchanged during revalue. refreshReport adds a certified rational upper reserve to the current incremental report. It does not subtract already recorded cash again. V07 then uses V06's same receipt operation and returns its Processed result. The next report can feed another update, so the interface supports repeated cycles.

The proof-assistant story for the talk

The progression demonstrates several distinct uses of proofs in software:

  1. Contracts tied to results. Safe (receive b paid paid₊) describes the particular computed state, rather than a detached arithmetic fact.
  2. Quantified regressions. A client projection, singleton stress family, zero MTA, and unchanged market each give a checked compatibility statement.
  3. Refinement at the numeric boundary. Rational execution is connected to real valuation by an explicit bound or equality. Finite stress enumeration alone does not give a decision procedure for arbitrary real order.
  4. Refinement at the event boundary. The identified-event state projects to the same economic state as crediting only newly received amounts.
  5. Dependent state changes. V06 transports financial evidence along that state equality. Its replay theorem identifies the engine state together with its safety certificate, even if the proof is reconstructed differently.

V01 and V04 also exhibit univalence paths between equivalent policy propositions. Cauchy reals are supplied by the existing higher-inductive construction; finite lattice folds avoid selecting a maximizing index. Each module uses --safe --cubical. The development adds no postulate or missing analysis axiom. The public operational computation from V04 onward compares rationals; it does not verify IEEE floating-point arithmetic or external pricing implementations.

Scope of this presentation model

Claims may come from swap portfolios or another instrument model. The engine consumes their valuation profiles, keeping instrument pricing outside this progression. Eligible collateral is a nonnegative supplied profile; the earlier Broker collateral allocation theory can justify such values from inventory, prices, and haircuts.

The catalog denotes distinct actual receipts with fixed owners and amounts. Its cash remains available at par and is separate from eligible collateral. The ledger proof does not authenticate a bank receipt, detect one physical payment assigned two different IDs, or provide durable storage. V06's state projection represents catalog-derived rational cash balances, not every V05 real cash state. There are no withdrawals, reversals, collateral returns, or dynamic catalog extension in this version sequence.

The revaluation bounds and stress family are model inputs. The loss is positive unsecured replacement exposure weighted by supplied default/recovery severity. Settlement principal, native-currency funding, liquidation execution, legal rights and empirical calibration require separate specifications. MTA is an explicit tolerated residual, with the whole gap requested only when it exceeds MTA; it is not a calibrated initial-margin or regulatory implementation.

certified-state-replay concerns receipt replay at a fixed valuation and pairs state with Guarantee. It does not assert that all conservative Report values are equal, or that an arbitrary revaluation workflow can be repeated unchanged.
