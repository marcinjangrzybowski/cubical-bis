# Fundamental theorem of calculus — 2026-09-15

See [Integration and FTC consequences](INTEGRATION_2026-09-15.md) for the
subsequent order, uniform-limit, series, oriented integral, integration by
parts, and substitution results.

The new results use `Cubical.Analysis.Real.Base.ℝ` and its semantic tagged
Riemann integral. They are exported by
[`Real.Integration.FundamentalTheorem`](Real/Integration/FundamentalTheorem.agda),
`Real.Integration`, and `Cubical.Analysis`.

## Integration of a derivative

[`fundamentalTheoremOfCalculusSecond`](Real/Integration/FundamentalTheorem/Second.agda)
proves

```agda
IsRadiuswiseUniformDerivativeOn F f a b →
HasRiemannIntegralOn f a b (F b - F a)
```

The hypothesis is the existing uniform first-order remainder estimate, with
one propositionally truncated radius per error tolerance. No continuity or
separate integrability assumption on `f` is required. For each cell, expand
`F` at the tag toward both endpoints. The two error bounds sum to the cell
width; summing the increments telescopes to `F b - F a`.

`uniformDerivativeIsRiemannIntegrable` constructs the integral witness.
`integralOfUniformDerivative` identifies any supplied integral with that value
when `a ≤ℝ b`. `uniformDerivativeIsIntegrableOnSubintervals` supplies all the
ordered subinterval integrals of such a derivative.

## Differentiation of an integral

[`UniformlyContinuousAccumulation`](Real/Integration/FundamentalTheorem/UniformContinuity.agda)
takes `f`, ordered endpoints, and standard uniform continuity on the closed
interval. It constructs all subinterval integrals and exposes `value`,
`hasValue`, `hasDerivative`, `hasUniformDerivative`, and
`valueOfUniformAntiderivative` with no separate integrability assumption.

[`Accumulation`](Real/Integration/FundamentalTheorem/Accumulation.agda) takes
`f`, ordered endpoints `a ≤ℝ b`, and `IsIntegrableOnSubintervals f a b`.
Its `value` is the actual function `x ↦ ∫[a,x] f` on `ClosedInterval a b`.

- `hasValue` states its semantic integral equation.
- `hasDerivative` proves its derivative is `f` from pointwise continuity on
  the closed interval.
- `hasUniformDerivative` gives the radiuswise uniform version from
  radiuswise uniform continuity on the closed interval.
- `valueOfUniformAntiderivative` proves `value x = F x - F a` for any
  radiuswise uniform antiderivative `F`.

These are derivatives relative to the interval, so the endpoint statements
are one-sided. No continuity or derivative assumptions outside the interval
are needed.

The underlying [`First`](Real/Integration/FundamentalTheorem/First.agda)
module works with `HasIntegralIncrementsOn F f a b`, the semantic statement
that every ordered subinterval integral equals the corresponding increment
of `F`. It exposes pointwise and uniform first theorems, plus
`uniformDerivative≃integralIncrements` when `f` is radiuswise uniformly
continuous on the interval. Lattice bounds using `minℝ` and `maxℝ` handle
both approach directions without deciding the order of arbitrary reals.

## Integrability from uniform continuity

[`uniformContinuityIsRiemannIntegrableOn`](Real/Integration/RiemannIntegral/UniformContinuity.agda)
proves

```agda
∀ f a b → a ≤ℝ b →
isUniformlyContinuous (ClosedIntervalPremetricSpace a b .snd)
  (f ∘ fst) (ℝPremetricSpace .snd) → IsRiemannIntegrableOn f a b
```

The standard `isUniformlyContinuous` predicate asserts the propositionally
truncated existence of one modulus. The theorem also has an explicit-modulus
version and a convenience version for globally uniformly continuous functions.
`uniformContinuityIsIntegrableOnSubintervals` restricts the same continuity
hypothesis to each ordered subinterval.

The proof compares arbitrary tagged sums using the lengths of overlapping
cells. The overlap weights sum to each original cell width in either
direction. Where two cells overlap, their tags are within the sum of their
widths; where they do not overlap, the weight is zero. Closed real inequality
case rules suffice, without selecting an ordering or a sorted common refinement.

An explicit dyadic mesh modulus composes with the continuity modulus to
construct the native `SeqWitness`. The existing sequential completion gives
its limit. Pairwise comparison then proves convergence of every sufficiently
fine tagged sum to this limit, which is the semantic integral.

The weaker radiuswise continuity predicate already implies
`IsRiemannCauchyOn`; `riemannCauchyLimitIsIntegral` identifies any supplied
vanishing-mesh sequence limit with the integral. Neither theorem promotes
separately truncated radii to a native Cauchy witness.

## Supporting results

- Interval-local absolute bounds for tagged sums and integrals. Existing
  global-bound signatures remain available as specializations.
- Concatenation of adjacent tagged partitions, preservation of mesh bounds,
  and exact addition of their Riemann sums, including zero-cell partitions.
- `riemannIntegral-concatenate` and `riemannIntegral-difference` for three
  existing integrals on adjacent ordered intervals. Two fixed dyadic
  sequences and uniqueness of limits suffice; no sequence of choices is made.

## Remaining work

Integrability from only radiuswise uniform continuity is still open. The
general `Accumulation` module retains its subinterval-integrability hypothesis;
`UniformlyContinuousAccumulation` now discharges it from standard uniform
continuity. No global modulus is selected from radiuswise estimates. Ordered
integrability is a proposition because each integral is unique.

The integration direction assumes uniform differentiability, not merely
pointwise differentiability. No automatic passage between these hypotheses
is claimed. The new `IntervalIntegral.between` API handles signed integrals
inside a fixed enclosing interval. The legacy raw integral predicate remains
vacuous on reversed intervals; it has not been reinterpreted as a signed integral.
